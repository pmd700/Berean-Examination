| Passage     | Paul’s wording                                                                     | What it means                                                                                        |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **7:1**     | “Now concerning the things about which you wrote…”                                 | Paul is answering Corinthian questions, not writing an abstract marriage manual.                     |
| **7:6**     | “By way of concession, not of command”                                             | Some of his instruction is permission/advice, not a binding command.                                 |
| **7:7**     | “I wish…” / “each has his own gift from God”                                       | Paul personally values singleness, but does not universalize it.                                     |
| **7:10**    | “Not I, but the Lord”                                                              | Here Paul appeals to a direct command from Christ about marriage/divorce.                            |
| **7:12**    | “I say, not the Lord”                                                              | Paul applies apostolic wisdom to a situation Jesus did not directly address in His earthly teaching. |
| **7:25**    | “I have no command of the Lord, but I give direction…”                             | No direct command from Jesus on this case, but Paul gives trustworthy apostolic counsel.             |
| **7:26**    | “In view of the present distress”                                                  | His advice is shaped by their current crisis or pressure.                                            |
| **7:28**    | “If you marry, you have not sinned”                                                | Paul is not forbidding marriage.                                                                     |
| **7:35**    | “For your own benefit, not to put a restraint on you”                              | Paul’s goal is pastoral help, not unnecessary restriction.                                           |
| **7:36–37** | “Let him do what he wishes… not under constraint… has authority over his own will” | Paul allows freedom of judgment in marriage decisions.                                               |
| **7:40**    | “In my opinion… I also have the Spirit of God”                                     | Paul’s opinion is not random; it is Spirit-guided apostolic judgment.                                |
